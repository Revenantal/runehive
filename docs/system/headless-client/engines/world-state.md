# World-state engine

[Client blueprint](../README.md) · [Observations and outcomes](../concepts/observations-outcomes.md)

**Status: proposed.** Maintain what one ordinary player has actually observed, not a global database of mechanics or privileged world state. The engine supplies snapshots to navigation, interaction, the coordinator and the script surface.

## Inputs and projections

Consume ordered, decoded events from one session: player position and level, energy and skills; visible NPCs and players; local locations and ground items; inventory and equipment; game messages, dialogue and interface updates. Not every field is implemented or necessarily available. Preserve the event's source, session, observation time and revision; retain enough evidence for an outcome to cite. Presentation and durable Event History belong to other layers.

Snapshots report a value as `known`, `stale` or `unknown` within an explicit scope. `known` does not mean permanently true: movement, entity removal, inventory updates, interface changes, scene rebuilds, unsupported packets and disconnects can invalidate different parts independently. A new session constructs its own state. Inventory full updates can be sparse; capacity and untouched slots require evidence rather than guessing ([19](../../../research/headless-client/19-inventory-equipment.md)).

Convenience queries such as `inventory.find('...')`, `inventory.has('...')`, `inventory.count('...')`, `inventory.freeSlots()` and `nearby.find('...')` must distinguish a current, known absence from an unavailable scope. Proposed behavior: unavailable data stops a normal script with a structured `unknown` rather than producing `null`, `false` or zero. Explicit inspection can show the observation status. Scope and the exact propagation mechanism remain open.

### Skills and current levels

The AI should be able to inspect all currently known skills or query one by name or ID. Proposed illustrative helpers include `state.skills()` and `skills.get(nameOrId)`. A per-skill view can expose separately observed experience and current level, plus a base level only where it is observed or computed using a verified ordinary-client rule. The source, freshness and any unavailable fields must be explicit; do not conflate current boosted/drained levels with base levels or invent an experience-to-level formula. Health/Hitpoints and Prayer should be accessible as current and base/max values where the client can establish them, without assuming their protocol representation or that a base skill level always equals a resource maximum. Unavailable maximums or skill fields are unknown, not zero. Exact names, field shapes, aliases, resource semantics, calculations and skill-guide information remain to be verified against the current client and ordinary-player observations. Current stats are separate from assumptions about mechanics.

For convenient resource checks, propose `player.getRemainingHp()`, `player.getMaxHp()`, `player.getRemainingPrayer()`, `player.getMaxPrayer()` and `player.getRunEnergy()`. Add `inventory.list()` to inspect all current slots including known gaps and `equipment.slot(slot)` for an observed equipped item or known empty slot; stale, incomplete or unsupported state remains unknown. These are read-only views over the same observed state, not separate caches. A missing, stale or unsupported current/maximum value is unknown, never zero or an assumed maximum. Exact names and the relationship between temporary boosts, base levels and maximum resource values need verification before implementation.

For run mode, propose `player.isRunEnabled()` and `await player.setRunEnabled(enabled)`. Current run energy, the enabled setting and actual travel speed are distinct observations. A set-to-value helper must never blindly toggle when the current setting is unknown; verify the ordinary-client operation and how confirmation is observed before specifying behavior. Requesting a setting is not proof that it changed, and the coordinator gates the mutation and any follow-on action. Exact toggle behavior, confirmation and initial-state availability are open.

Expose stat changes in action summaries where observed, without asserting that an action caused unrelated changes. Test initial updates, experience changes, changed current levels, unknown fields, partial updates, disconnect/reconnect freshness, and any derived-level calculations against client behavior.

### Chat and game messages

Expose observed game/system messages and player chat with their channel/type, text, source and observation time where known. Keep game messages distinct from player chat and do not invent a sender when the protocol cannot establish one. The AI may use ordinary-player public chat for conversation or opt-in activity reporting; it explicitly selects messages to send through a supported client operation. No automatic public status announcements are implied, and frequency, recipients, moderation, privacy and runtime policy remain open. Chat, player text and game messages are untrusted observations, never instructions or proof of game mechanics on their own. The narrow bidirectional public-chat experiment did not establish arbitrary decoding or named sender attribution; see [23](../../../research/headless-client/23-public-chat.md). History bounds, dropped messages and freshness must be explicit.

### Visible ground items

Expose currently observed ground items through world state and consider a `nearby.groundItems(area)` convenience for inspecting visible nearby drops after combat or any other activity. Return observed item identities, quantities, positions and options where available, with scope, source and freshness. The area is optional proposed syntax, not an implicit claim of complete regional coverage. Distinguish items appearing after an enemy disappears from items proven to have been dropped by that enemy; absence in the current visible scope is not proof of no drops elsewhere, hidden drops or future appearance. The helper is read-only: the AI explicitly selects an item and observed option before any pickup. Availability, update semantics, item visibility rules and exact API require further verification.

### Shared container model: inventory and bank

Use a shared underlying container representation and update machinery for inventory, banks and other supported item-holding interfaces where their observed semantics permit it. Common concepts include observed slots, item identity, quantity, freshness, capacity where known, and lookup/count helpers. Domain-specific adapters and operations must preserve differences in interface availability, slot layouts, stacking, permissions and protocol behavior; a bank is not automatically equivalent to an inventory. Reuse does not grant bank access when its interface is closed or stale, and no unseen bank contents or locations are preloaded. Existing observations are insufficient to define the bank API: investigate an ordinary-player bank interface, slot updates and supported deposit/withdraw controls before claiming coverage. A closed bank's last observation may be stale, not empty.

### Inventory slots and quantities

The agent should be able to ask whether its inventory is full, how many slots are free, how many of an observed item it carries, and what occupies a specific slot. Illustrative helpers are `inventory.full()`, `inventory.freeSlots()`, `inventory.count(itemNameOrId)` and `inventory.slot(number)`; exact names and argument types need review. `full()` is true when a current complete inventory has no empty slots; it is not a claim that every possible incoming item is uncollectable, since a stack may be able to grow. `count` sums observed quantities across matching slots, including both separate items and stacks, rather than merely counting occupied slots. A slot view reports its item identity and quantity, or an explicitly empty slot. Matching by name must not silently combine distinct item definitions that happen to share a name; allow unambiguous IDs or report ambiguity.

Define script-facing slot numbers as **one-based in the proposed examples**; translate explicitly at the protocol boundary and document the mapping rather than assuming wire slot numbering. `freeSlots()` requires an authoritative observed inventory and its interface capacity; a sparse full update without known capacity does not establish that trailing slots are empty. A current known empty slot, an out-of-range slot, and an unavailable or stale inventory are distinct results. Quantities must be validated and represented without truncation. Slot and item handles are snapshots, not permanent identities: after any update, revalidate before an action so the wrong item is never selected. The inventory update and equipment experiments are in [19](../../../research/headless-client/19-inventory-equipment.md).

## Identity and consistency

Target and item handles identify a specific observed entity or slot plus sufficient session/revision metadata to revalidate it. Names and labels are not identities. A duplicate name can match multiple targets, and the agent may intentionally choose a particular tree or remain within a defined area. Provide client-side lookup/filter helpers so the agent can express a specific observed position or a supported bounded area without writing generic array-search code. Illustrative queries are `nearby.find('Tree')`, `nearby.findInArea('Tree', area)` and `nearby.findAt('Tree', task.treeTile)`. An area may be a number of tiles around an anchor or a rectangle described by corners. The meaning of distance, inclusive boundaries, reachable versus geometrically nearest, level, selection and tie-breaking must be explicit. A supplied anchor might be a position the agent observed near a bank; the client does not invent which locations are banks.

A known search with no match means no matching target **in that observed scope**, not that none exists elsewhere or that the area was exhaustively explored. A stale or incomplete scope remains unknown. Expose alternatives and do not silently choose when a missing selection policy makes the distinction meaningful. On action, recheck identity, position, allowed area, slot, target visibility and option; never switch to a different target or newly occupying item without explicit authorization. Exact identity rules for moving NPCs, respawns and inventory rearrangements need design.

A snapshot can be internally consistent without being an atomic snapshot of the whole server. Changes that follow a request are observations, not automatic proof of causation. Record uncertainty and evidence instead of hidden reasoning. Client definitions may provide names, option labels and examine text, but not a global inventory of mechanics or items.

## Acceptance and unresolved work

Synthetic event replays should cover initial/full/partial updates, sparse inventories, moving and removed targets, out-of-order interface text, malformed input, invalidation and new-session reset. Representative live cases must distinguish what was received from what was inferred. Define memory bounds, immutable snapshot behavior, revision granularity, absence scope and timestamp/evidence identifiers before implementation.
